\section{Convex Neighborhoods}\label{ch09:s4}


This section is devoted to proving the following theorem, due to J. H. C. Whitehead \cite{whitehead1932convex}. 



\begin{theorem} \label{thm:ch9.4.1}
Let $M$ be a $\CInfty$ manifold and $\connection$ be a $\CInfty$ connexion on $M$. Then for any point $m$ in $M$ there is a neighbourhood $U$ of $m$ that is convex; i.e., for any two points in $U$ there is a unique geodesic of $\connection$ which joins the two points and lies in $U$. \index{convex neighborhood}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We may assume $\connection$ has zero torsion, since by section \ref{ch05:s4} there is a unique torsion-free connexion with the same geodesics. The theorem is local, and we work completely in one coordinate neighbourhood of $m$. From the previous section, we choose a normal coordinate system $x_1, x_2, \dots, x_n$ about $m$ with domain $A$, thus $x_i(m) = 0$ and $\Gamma^i_{jk}(m) = 0$ for all $i, j$ and $k$. Let 
\begin{itemize}
    \item $d(p,q)$ be a local metric on $A$ defined by \[d(p,q) = \sqrt{\sum_{i} (x_i(p) - x_i(q))^2},\]
    \item $f(p) = d(p,m)$,
    \item $B(p,c) = \{q\in A: d(p,q)<c\}$ for $p\in A$.
    \item $\norm{X} = \sqrt{\displaystyle \sum_{i} (\dd{x_i}(X)^2)} $ for $p\in A$ and $X\in \tangentspace{M}{p}$.
\end{itemize}

By Corollary \ref{cor:ch9.3.3}, $G(q,X)=(q,\exp_qX)$ has a local inverse near each $(p,p)$. Fix $c>0$ with $K=\xoverline B(m,c)\subset A$. Compactness of the diagonal over $K$ lets us choose $s>0$ uniformly so that, for each $p\in K$, the inverse branch through $(p,0)$ is defined on $\{p\}\times B(p,s)$. Restricting this branch gives a diffeomorphism $\exp_p:\xoverline U_p\functionMaps B(p,s)$. \emph{Editor's note:} We use local inverse branches near the zero section, not all preimages under $\exp$; the original also incorrectly calls its positive radii integers and uses $\exp_pX$ for a vector based at $q$. We have proved:


\begin{lemma}\label{lem:ch9.4.1a}
For any $c>0$ with $\xoverline{B}(m,c)\subset A$, there exists an $s>0$ such that for $p\in \xoverline{B}(m,c)$ the map $\exp_p$ is a diffeomorphism from a neighbourhood $\xoverline{U}_p$ of 0 in $\tangentspace{M}{p}$ onto $B(p,s)\subset A$.

We now prove two lemmas that complete the proof of the theorem. 
\end{lemma}


\begin{lemma} \label{lem:ch9.4.1b}
There exists a real number $a$, $0<a<1$ and $\xoverline{B}(m,a)\subset A$, such that if $0<b<a$ and $g$ is a nonconstant geodesic with tangent $T$ and $f\circ g(0)=b$, $T_{g(0)} f = 0$, then $f\circ g$ has a strict relative minimum at $0$. Thus if $g$ is tangent to the ``sphere about $m$ of radius $b$'' at $g(0)$, then $g$ lies outside of $B(m,b)$ near $g(0)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
We may assume $\norm{T_{g(0)}}=1$. Let $T=\displaystyle\sum_j a_j X_j$ where $X_j = \pdv{}{x_j}$ and \newline $a_j\circ g = \Big(\dv{}{t}\Big)(x_j\circ g)$, and we assume $T_{g(0)}$ is extended to a $\CInfty$ field in a neighbourhood of $g(0)$. Since $f = \sqrt{\displaystyle\sum_i x_i^2}$ we have 
\[Tf=\sum_j a_j (X_j f) = \dfrac{1}{f} \sum_j a_j x_j\] and 
\[T^2 f = \sum_k a_k\bigg[-\dfrac{x_k}{f^3}\bigg(\sum_j a_jx_j\bigg) + \dfrac{1}{f}\bigg(\sum_j (X_ka_j)x_j+a_k\bigg)\bigg].\] At $t=0$, or at $g(0)$, $Tf = 0$; hence
\[T^2 f = \dfrac{1}{b} \bigg[\sum_k a_k^2 + \sum_{k, j} a_kx_j (X_k a_j)\bigg]\] But at $g(0)$,
\[\sum_k a_k^2 = \norm{T}^2 = 1\text{ and }\sum_k a_k(X_k a_j) + \sum_{r,s} \Gamma^j_{rs} a_r a_s = 0,\] since $g$ is a geodesic. Thus 
\[T^2 f = \dfrac{1}{b}\bigg[1-\sum_{j,r,s} x_j\Gamma^j_{rs} a_r a_s\bigg].\] Choose $a>0$ and $a<1$ so for points $p$ with $f(p)\le a$, $\norm{\Gamma^i_{jk} (p)}<1/2n^3$ for all $i$, $j$ and $k$, which is possible since $\Gamma^i_{jk}$ continuous and $\Gamma^i_{jk}(m) = 0$. Then, at $g(0)$,
\[
\bigg|\sum_{j,r,s} x_j \Gamma^j_{rs} a_r a_s\bigg| \le \Big(\frac{1}{2n^3}\Big) \bigg(\sum_{j,r,s} 1\bigg) \le \frac{1}{2},
\]
hence $T^2 f(g(0)) > 0$, which implies $f\circ g$ has a strict relative minimum at 0. 
\end{proof}


\begin{lemma} \label{lem:ch9.4.1c}
Let $a$ be given by Lemma \ref{lem:ch9.4.1b}. Apply Lemma \ref{lem:ch9.4.1a} with $c=a/2$ and decrease $s>0$ as necessary. Then $B(m,s/2)$ is convex.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For sufficiently small $s$, the inverse branch of $G$ near $(m,m)$ determines a geodesic $g_{p,q}(t)$, $0\leq t\leq1$, joining each $p,q\in B(m,s/2)$. Smooth dependence on $(p,q,t)$ and compactness of $[0,1]$ let us decrease $s$ so every such geodesic lies in $B(m,a)$. The function $f\circ g_{p,q}$ attains its maximum. If this maximum were at least $s/2$, it would occur at an interior parameter, since the endpoints lie in $B(m,s/2)$. Its value is less than $a$, so Lemma \ref{lem:ch9.4.1b} would make it a strict local minimum, a contradiction. Thus this geodesic lies in $B(m,s/2)$.

For completeness, uniqueness applies to every geodesic lying in this ball, not merely the chosen short branch. In the fixed chart choose $C$ such that a geodesic satisfies $|x''|\leq C|x'|^2$ on $\xoverline B(m,a)$. Its Euclidean unit tangent changes by at most $2C$ per unit Euclidean arc length. If a geodesic confined to a ball of radius $r=s/2$ had length at least $4r$, then, for $r$ sufficiently small, its first $4r$ of arc length would have tangent making scalar product at least $1/2$ with the initial tangent. Its displacement would be at least $2r$, impossible in the open ball. Hence its length $\ell<4r$. Also $|\dd\log|x'|/\dd\ell|\leq C$, so, when its parameter interval is $[0,1]$, $|x'(0)|\leq\ell e^{C\ell}$. Decreasing $r$ once more puts all these initial vectors in the injectivity neighborhood of $G$ near $(m,0)$. Therefore every geodesic confined to the ball equals the chosen inverse-branch geodesic. \emph{Editor's note:} The original assumes, without justification from Lemma \ref{lem:ch9.4.1a}, that its joining geodesic stays in $B(p,s)$. The confinement and uniqueness arguments above supply the missing steps.
\end{proof}


Our theorem follows.
\end{proof}
